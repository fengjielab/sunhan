\begin{abstract}
Fixed teleoperation settings cannot readily accommodate objects with different fragility, geometry, and grasping requirements. Existing object-conditioned approaches primarily adapt slave-side impedance, while coordinated configuration of the slave response, operator-facing haptic interface, and gripper execution remains less explored. This paper presents an object-conditioned framework that maps object semantics to a common operational strategy and jointly configures these three mechatronic subsystems. Low-rate visual perception is integrated with a nominal 200-Hz supervisory loop through asynchronous, non-blocking communication, and the first valid detection triggers a one-shot parameter update that remains fixed for the trial. Three operators completed 135 grasp-and-transfer trials involving six objects and five control modes. Compared with impedance-only scheduling, the fully coordinated mode reduced mean completion time by 1.79 s, corresponding to 8.5\%, with a matched-block bootstrap 95\% confidence interval of [1.10, 2.51] s. The direction of the difference was consistent across all operators and objects, while the coordinated mode also showed the highest observed success rate and the lowest Raw NASA-TLX score. These results provide system-level evidence that jointly configuring the haptic interface and gripper with slave-side impedance can improve heterogeneous-object teleoperation under the present experimental conditions.

\end{abstract}
