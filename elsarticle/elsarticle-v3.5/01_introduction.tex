\section{Introduction}

Teleoperation combines human judgment with robotic execution, enabling remote robots to perform complex manipulation tasks that remain challenging for fully autonomous systems, including hazardous operations, flexible manufacturing, and manipulation in unstructured environments. Haptic feedback conveys remote contact information to the operator and facilitates assessment of contact state, grasp stability, and object slip. A practical teleoperation system therefore requires the effective integration of bilateral control, environmental perception, robotic execution, and human--machine interaction \cite{ref01,ref02}.

Reliable teleoperation becomes particularly challenging when heterogeneous objects are involved. Such objects may differ substantially in geometry, deformability, contact sensitivity, and grasp-stability requirements, making a single fixed parameter setting inadequate for achieving both compliant contact and stable manipulation. For example, lower robot stiffness and grasp force can reduce contact impact when handling fragile or deformable objects, but may compromise positioning accuracy and grasp stability. Conversely, more aggressive parameter settings may improve robustness when manipulating rigid or slippery objects, while increasing the risk of collision or object deformation. Object categories can therefore provide compact semantic descriptions of interaction requirements, supporting object-dependent parameter selection rather than a universal configuration for different objects.

Impedance control provides a fundamental means of regulating physical interaction by shaping the relationship among robot position, velocity, and interaction force \cite{ref03}. Variable-impedance methods have been extensively investigated to improve robot adaptability by adjusting stiffness and damping according to task state, contact information, human behavior, or learned policies \cite{ref04,ref05}. In teleoperation, time-varying impedance and bilateral-control methods have further explored the regulation of remote-robot behavior based on operator motion, estimated human impedance, contact state, task phase, and environmental interaction \cite{ref06,ref07,ref08,ref09,ref18,ref19,ref20,ref21}. These studies have established important approaches for improving remote compliance, stability, and transparency.

Meanwhile, advances in visual and multimodal perception have enabled teleoperation systems to exploit object- and task-related information when supporting control decisions. Previous studies have employed visual information, force feedback, speech, gaze, and other modalities to select task-dependent impedance parameters, generate interaction strategies, or reduce the operator's decision-making burden \cite{ref10,ref11,ref12,ref13,ref14,ref15,ref16}. Tactile and kinesthetic feedback, as well as grip-safety information, has also been investigated to improve operator awareness and grasp execution \cite{ref17,ref22,ref25,ref30}. These studies demonstrate that object-level semantic information can provide valuable guidance for parameter selection and strategy adaptation in teleoperation.

Despite these advances, existing approaches predominantly focus on adapting an individual aspect of the teleoperation system, particularly robot-side impedance or control-authority allocation. However, teleoperation performance depends not only on the physical interaction between the remote robot and its environment, but also on the information presented to the operator and the execution behavior of the end effector. Robot impedance governs contact compliance, master-side haptic feedback influences the operator's perception of remote interaction, and gripper parameters directly affect grasp execution. These factors act at different stages of the manipulation process but play complementary roles in determining task efficiency, interaction safety, and grasp stability. Accordingly, object-level semantic information can be used to establish an interpretable manipulation strategy and provide an appropriate initial configuration for multiple task-related channels, reducing the need for repeated online parameter adjustment while preserving operator involvement.

Directly incorporating visual semantic inference into the real-time control loop, however, introduces an additional integration challenge. Visual inference generally operates at a lower update rate and may be affected by computational latency and detection fluctuations, whereas the teleoperation loop must remain continuously responsive to the operator. Allowing every perception result to trigger immediate parameter changes may delay supervisory updates, induce repeated switching, and produce inconsistent interaction behavior. A practical architecture should therefore convert visual semantic information into a stable task-level strategy without placing visual inference directly in the real-time control path.

To address these issues, this paper proposes an \textbf{object-conditioned multi-channel strategy-configuration framework} for haptic teleoperation of heterogeneous objects in semi-structured manipulation scenarios. The detected object class is interpreted according to its task-relevant interaction requirements and mapped to one of three manipulation strategies: \textit{fragility-priority}, \textit{balanced}, or \textit{stability-priority}. The selected strategy is subsequently realized through multiple task-related channels, including slave-side robot impedance, master-side haptic feedback, and gripper execution parameters. The mapping provides an interpretable intermediate layer between visual recognition and teleoperation parameter configuration and uses engineering-selected operating points for the tested object classes and platform.

Unlike continuous online adaptation methods, the proposed framework performs a single strategy-configuration event at task initiation and maintains the selected strategy throughout the subsequent manipulation. This design decouples low-rate visual semantic inference from the high-rate teleoperation loop, avoids repeated parameter switching caused by detection fluctuations, and maintains configuration consistency while preserving operator authority during task execution.

The proposed framework is evaluated through a five-mode human-in-the-loop experiment conducted on an Omega.7--Franka Panda teleoperation platform. The experiment involves three operators, six heterogeneous objects, and 135 trials. Task completion time is the primary endpoint. The primary C--E comparison evaluates the additional system-level benefit of the complete multi-channel configuration relative to impedance-only reconfiguration under matched visual-semantic information and slave-side impedance updating, while the C--D comparison examines the benefit beyond visual-semantic display alone. The C--B comparison provides an automated-workflow comparison with manual strategy selection included in the timed procedure.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Interpretable object-to-strategy mapping method.} An interpretable mapping is established between object perception and teleoperation control. Object characteristics are interpreted in terms of deformation tolerance, contact sensitivity, and grasp-stability requirements, and mapped to high-level manipulation strategies rather than directly to fixed parameter values. These strategies guide the subsequent configuration of slave-side impedance, master-side haptic rendering, and gripper execution.
\item \textbf{Non-blocking multi-channel strategy deployment.} A non-blocking architecture realizes the selected strategy through slave-side impedance, master-side haptic feedback, and gripper execution parameters. One-shot configuration and task-level strategy locking decouple low-rate visual inference from the high-rate teleoperation loop, avoid repeated parameter switching, and maintain configuration consistency throughout each manipulation task.
\item \textbf{Matched human-in-the-loop comparative evaluation.} A five-mode experimental design involving three operators, six heterogeneous objects, and 135 trials evaluates the task-level benefit of semantic-assisted multi-channel strategy configuration relative to fixed-parameter operation, manual strategy selection, visual-semantic display, and impedance-only reconfiguration.
\end{enumerate}

The framework is intended for semi-structured teleoperation scenarios in which the platform and workspace are fixed while object-level interaction requirements vary. Within this setting, it provides a semantic-assisted strategy-configuration approach for improving the consistency and efficiency of heterogeneous-object teleoperation.
